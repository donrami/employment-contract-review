# EU Law Layer & Transposition — Employment-Contract Assessment Reference (Germany)

**As of: 2026-08-22.** All legal bases verified against EUR-Lex, gesetze-im-internet.de, Bundestag documents, Curia and practitioner commentary; source URLs given inline. Items marked **[unverified]** could not be confirmed from primary sources in this research pass. Everything else is verified.

**Top-line status flags for the skill (2026-08):**
- **Pay Transparency Directive (EU) 2023/970: transposition deadline (7 June 2026) MISSED by Germany.** No Referentenentwurf as of late May 2026; earliest national act realistically end-2026/2027. Directive nonetheless bites from 8 June 2026: directly binding on public employers; via Art. 157 TFEU, Art. 7(6) (pay-disclosure bans void), Art. 4 (objective, gender-neutral pay structures) and duty of conforming interpretation for private employers.
- **Minimum Wage Directive (EU) 2022/2041: partially annulled** by CJEU 11.11.2025, C-19/23 (Denmark v Parliament and Council): Art. 5(2)/(4) criteria for setting/updating statutory minimum wages void; rest of directive survives; MiLoG unaffected; German minimum wage €13.90 (1.1.2026) / €14.60 (1.1.2027).
- **ArbZG 8h daily cap: reform pending.** June 2026: Labour Minister Bas announced draft bill to replace daily 8h limit (§ 3 ArbZG) with weekly cap; § 5 ArbZG 11h rest and EU 48h weekly average (Art. 6 Directive 2003/88) remain binding EU floor. Not yet enacted as of 2026-08.
- **Platform Work Directive (EU) 2024/2831: transposition deadline 2.12.2026.** BMAS Referentenentwurf in preparation (Bundestag answer 24.4.2026); no published draft as of 2026-08.
- **July 2026 coalition reform package ("Aufschwung und Beschäftigung")** — not yet enacted: fixed-term without objective reason up to 48 months (currently 24) and up to 6 renewals for hires until end-2030; KSchG relaxation for high earners (2027); sick-note from day 1; codetermination/AI items. Watch: relaxation of § 14(2) TzBfG would collide with CJEU fixed-term case law on abuse (Adeneler; Grupo Norte).

## 1. Directives directly relevant to employment contracts — basis & German transposition

### 1.1 Working time — Directive 2003/88/EC → ArbZG
- **Legal basis:** Art. 153(1)(a) TFEU. Core: max weekly average 48h incl. overtime (Art. 6); min daily rest 11 consecutive hours (Art. 3); min weekly rest 35h incl. daily rest (Art. 5); breaks (Art. 4); at least 4 weeks paid annual leave (Art. 7(1)).
- **German transposition:** Arbeitszeitgesetz (ArbZG). § 3: max 8h/day, extendable to 10h if average over 6 months or 24 weeks ≤ 8h (this is the 10h/48h-average structure; EU's 48h weekly average floors German law, German law is stricter at daily level). § 4: breaks; § 5: 11h daily rest; § 6a: night work 8h/10h cap; § 7: collective-agreement derogations, all bounded by EU minimums.
- **Recording obligation:** CJEU C-55/18 (CCOO, 14.5.2019): Member States must require employers to set up objective, reliable, accessible time-recording systems — Art. 3, 5, 6, 16(2) Directive 2003/88. BAG 13.9.2022 (1 ABR 22/21): employer's obligation to record working time derives from § 3(2)(1) ArbSchG (occupational health & safety), applies to all employers, all employees — no statutory amendment needed; electronic recording not (yet) mandated by BAG itself. **Reform status:** BMAS Referentenentwurf of 18.4.2023 (electronic recording) lapsed with the 2021–2025 Bundestag; no new draft published as of 2026-08; ministerial announcement of a new ArbZG bill (weekly cap + mandatory electronic recording) for June 2026, parliamentary deliberations autumn 2026; not law yet. Sources: [C-55/18 EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/DE/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62018CJ0055); [BAG 1 ABR 22/21](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/presse/arbeitszeiterfassung-ein-arbeitgeber-ist-nach-3-abs-2-satz-1-arbschg-verpflichtet-die-arbeitszeit-seiner-arbeitnehmer-zu-erfassen); [timetrackapp reform 2026](https://www.timetrackapp.com/blog/arbeitszeitgesetz-reform-2026); [kliemt BMAS draft 2023](https://kliemt.blog/2023/04/18/update-zur-arbeitszeiterfassung-bmas-legt-gesetzentwurf-vor).
- **On-call time (Bereitschaft/Rufbereitschaft):** CJEU C-518/15 (Matzak, 21.2.2018): standby at home with 8-minute response duty = working time under Directive 2003/88; C-344/19 + C-580/19 (Radiotelevizija Slovenija, 9.3.2021): on-call time counts as working time where constraints objectively and very significantly affect the worker's ability to manage free time — location requirement alone insufficient, intensity matters; C-214/20 (Dublin City Council, 11.11.2021): part-time firefighter standby where only occasional call-outs, free to pursue private life = rest time; confirms case-by-case intensity test. German § 7(1) No. 1a ArbZG allows derogation for on-call duty; § 5(3) allows rest-period shortening; German courts apply the Matzak/DJ intensity criteria. Sources: [C-518/15 dejure](https://dejure.org/dienste/vernetzung/rechtsprechung?Text=C-518%2F15); [C-344/19 dejure](https://dejure.org/dienste/vernetzung/rechtsprechung?Gericht=EuGH&Datum=09.03.2021&Aktenzeichen=C-344/19); [C-214/20 EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/DE/ALL/?uri=CELEX:62020CJ0214).
- **Travel time (NEW 2025):** CJEU C-110/24 (9.10.2025, Spain): employer-determined travel (company transport, fixed meeting point, set schedule) for workers with no fixed workplace = working time under Directive 2003/88 for safety purposes, regardless of whether the worker drives, works or rests. Not (yet) transposed into German case law; BAG continues to focus on burden (e.g. driving duty) for pay; employers should review tightly organized travel against ArbZG caps. Source: [Littler, 30.1.2026](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/travel-time-or-working-time-what-latest-cjeu-decision-means-employers-germany).

### 1.2 Part-time & fixed-term — Directives 97/81/EC and 99/70/EC → TzBfG
- **Basis:** Framework Agreement on part-time work (Annex Directive 97/81/EC), clause 4: non-discrimination vs comparable full-timer; Framework Agreement on fixed-term work (Annex Directive 99/70/EC), clause 5: measures to prevent successive fixed-term contracts (abuse), clause 4: non-discrimination.
- **German transposition:** Teilzeit- und Befristungsgesetz (TzBfG). § 2 (part-time definition), § 4(1) (non-discrimination vs comparable full-time), § 5 (pay/benefits pro-rata), § 7 (request to change hours; since 1.8.2022 reasoned written reply within 1 month — Art. 12 Directive 2019/1152 as well), § 9 (fixed-term), § 14(1) (objective justification, incl. statutory catalogue), **§ 14(2)**: fixed term without objective reason up to **2 years, max 3 renewals**, only where no prior employment with same employer (**Vorbeschäftigungsverbot**, § 14(2) s.2).
- **CJEU scrutiny of § 14(2) TzBfG:** No CJEU ruling directly on § 14(2) as of 2026-08; BAG upheld the provision's compatibility with clause 5 Framework Agreement (e.g. BAG 26.10.2016 – 7 AZR 140/15; BAG 23.1.2019 – 7 AZR 733/16 etc. on prior employment). CJEU fixed-term-abuse doctrine that constrains German practice: C-212/04 (Adeneler, 4.7.2006) — national courts must convert successive fixed-term contracts into indefinite ones and interpret national law to prevent abuse; C-574/16 (Grupo Norte, 14.9.2016) — national court must verify, of its own motion, that misuse has not occurred and apply effective sanctions. **[unverified]** — no pending CJEU reference specifically challenging § 14(2) TzBfG could be confirmed in this pass. The July 2026 reform package (48 months / 6 renewals for new hires until 2030) is a statutory draft only; if enacted, it will face clause-5 scrutiny.
- **§ 4 TzBfG discrimination ban:** mirrors clause 4 of both framework agreements; applies to pay and other working conditions; pro-rata temporis for part-timers.

### 1.3 Transparent & predictable working conditions — Directive (EU) 2019/1152 → NachwG 2022 amendments
- **Basis:** Directive (EU) 2019/1152 (ABl. L 186, 11.7.2019). Replaces Directive 91/533/EEC. Transposition deadline 1.8.2022.
- **German transposition:** Gesetz zur Umsetzung der Richtlinie (EU) 2019/1152 (Bundestag-Drs. 20/1636, 2.5.2022; in force 1.8.2022). Core changes:
  - **§ 2(1) NachwG** — expanded mandatory documentation (written form retained, no electronic form; Art. 3(2) Directive permits electronic, Germany deliberately did not use the option): (1) names/addresses of parties; (2) start date; (3) fixed-term end date or foreseeable duration; (4) place of work or freedom to choose workplace; (5) job description; (6) probation duration if agreed; (7) pay composition and amount, **overtime pay, bonuses, allowances, premiums, special payments separately itemized**, due date and method of payment; (8) agreed working time, rest breaks and rest periods, shift system/rhythm and change conditions; (9) on-call work (§ 12 TzBfG): agreement on demand work, minimum paid hours, time frame via reference days/hours, advance notice period; (10) overtime arrangement conditions if agreed; (11) entitlement to employer-provided training; (12) occupational pension provider name/address; (13) termination procedure, at least written-form requirement and statutory notice periods plus **3-week deadline for filing unfair-dismissal claim (§ 7 KSchG applies even if not properly documented)**; (14) collective agreements/Betriebsvereinbarungen in general form.
  - **Deadlines (§ 2(1) s.4 NachwG):** items 1, 7, 8 by first day of work; items 2–6, 9, 10 within 7 calendar days; remainder within 1 month.
  - **§ 3 NachwG:** notification of changes on the day they take effect (was 1 month).
  - **§ 4 NachwG (new):** fine up to **€2,000** for non/incorrect/incomplete/late documentation.
  - **§ 2(2)–(3) NachwG:** postings abroad > 4 consecutive weeks: country, duration, currency, allowances, return conditions; posting under Directive 96/71/EC as amended: host-state remuneration and link to official national website.
  - **§ 23c AEntG (new):** information duty for recruiting EU citizens resident abroad ("Faire Mobilität" referral).
  - **TzBfG amendments:** § 7(3) reasoned reply within 1 month to change-of-hours request (also § 18(2) to request for indefinite contract, >6 months employment); § 12(3) reference days/hours mandatory + 4-day advance notice; § 15(3) probation proportionate to fixed-term duration.
  - **§ 111 GewO / § 32a SeeArbG:** mandatory training costs not chargeable to employee; counts as working time if outside regular hours.
- **Exclusivity clauses (Art. 9 Directive 2019/1152 — ban on restrictions on parallel employment):** Germany did **not** enact an explicit statutory prohibition. The Bundestag draft (Drs. 20/1636) contains no exclusivity-clause ban; the transposition rationale holds that existing German law (contractual freedom, § 138/§ 242 BGB abuse control, § 307 BGB AGB review) already prevents unreasonable restraints, and parallel employment is generally lawful absent conflicting duties. Effect: exclusivity clauses are not per se void, but an absolute prohibition on any secondary employment without objective justification fails AGB review and loyalty-duty limits (BAG 18.11.1988 – 8 AZR 12/86; BAG 11.12.2001 – 9 AZR 464/00). The skill should flag absolute exclusivity bans as high-risk. Sources: [Bundestag Drs. 20/1636 PDF](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/20/016/2001636.pdf); [CMS legal update](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/Umsetzung-der-sog.-Arbeitsbedingungenrichtlinie-Arbeitgeber-muessen-handeln-Update-1); [IHK NachwG](https://www.ihk.de/wiesbaden/recht/arbeitsrecht-online/mitarbeiter-einstellen/aenderungen-nachweisgesetz-5605088).
- **Probation cap:** Directive 2019/1152 Art. 6 (max 6 months, derogations for managerial functions/young workers/fixed-term >6 months); German law: § 622(3) BGB (max 6 months), § 15(3) TzBfG proportionality for fixed-term; Germany did not adopt the directive's flexibility options (CMS, above).
- **Predictability of work schedules:** Art. 10 Directive 2019/1152 (right to request predictable working conditions) implemented via § 7 TzBfG and § 12 TzBfG (on-call).

### 1.4 Anti-discrimination — Directives 2000/78/EC, 2000/43/EC, 2006/54/EC → AGG
- **Basis:** 2000/78/EC (employment framework; religion, belief, disability, age, sexual orientation), 2000/43/EC (racial/ethnic origin — employment and beyond), 2006/54/EC (gender in employment/occupation incl. equal pay, pregnancy/maternity).
- **German transposition:** Allgemeines Gleichbehandlungsgesetz (AGG, 2006). § 1 protected grounds incl. race/ethnic origin, gender, religion/worldview, disability, age, sexual identity; § 7 prohibition of discrimination in employment incl. contractual terms; **§ 22 AGG burden of proof**: employee must establish prima facie indication (indicia); employer bears proof of no discrimination. **§ 15 AGG compensation** (up to 3 monthly salaries for non-hiring, no cap otherwise), **§ 16 AGG** (no retaliation).
- **CJEU dress-code/religion cases relevant to contract clauses:**
  - C-157/15 (Achbita, 14.3.2017): internal rule banning visible religious symbols in customer contact — indirect discrimination justified if applied generally/indifferently, pursues genuine employer need shown proportionately; prohibition may not be based on specific religious practice.
  - C-68/17 (JR, 14.3.2017): direct discrimination where rule targets a specific religion (e.g. headscarf ban formulated as such); only genuine occupational requirements (Art. 4(1) Directive 2000/78) can justify direct discrimination.
  - **Drafting impact:** dress-code/grooming clauses must be neutral on their face and justified by objective, proportional business reasons; any clause singling out religious garb is directly discriminatory. German labor courts follow (BAG 17.10.2019 – 6 AZR 145/18, post-Achbita).
- **Pregnancy:** C-177/88 (Dekker, 8.11.1990): refusal to hire due to pregnancy = direct sex discrimination; reinforced by 2006/54/EC Art. 2(2)(c); German protection: AGG + MuSchG (maternity protection) + KSchG § 9 MuSchG special dismissal ban.
- **Age:** C-411/05 (Palacios) & C-144/04 (Mangold) — age discrimination framework; C-341/08 (Petersen) — maximum ages for professions. German § 10 AGG permits objective age distinctions (e.g. retirement-age hiring) within narrow bounds.

### 1.5 Data protection in employment — GDPR + § 26 BDSG
- **Basis:** Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR) directly applicable; Art. 88 GDPR + Art. 7 (consent conditions). German implementing provision: **§ 26 BDSG** (processing of employee data for employment-relationship purposes; requires necessity, or consent per Art. 7 GDPR/§ 26(2) BDSG).
- **CJEU cases affecting contract clauses (monitoring/surveillance):**
  - C-34/21 (Österreichische Gesundheitskasse, 30.3.2023): Art. 88(1) GDPR + Art. 6(1)(c)/6(3) — national provisions on processing of health data of employees must be sufficiently specific; § 26(1) BDSG as general clause cannot justify special-category (health) data processing without more specific rules. Direct impact on German employment data processing clauses: consent or works-agreement basis alone insufficient for health data.
  - C-61/19 (Orange România, 11.11.2020) — [task's "biometric" attribution is **incorrect**]: consent validity for retention of ID copies in a telecom contract; pre-ticked/annexed consent not freely given. Applied to employment: consent embedded in contract documents is suspect; surveillance clauses relying on contract consent alone are weak.
  - **Biometric data in employment — the correct case is C-439/19 (B, 22.6.2021)**: member-state law (Latvia) requiring collection of fingerprint data for employment of public-sector candidates; CJEU: special-category data under Art. 9(2)(b) GDPR requires explicit consent; national law must itself establish necessity — but the ruling concerned *legal* mandates; for employer biometric systems (time recording, access control) consent under Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR + § 26(2) BDSG must be explicit and free; German supervisory authorities treat biometric access/time systems as high-risk (Art. 35 GDPR DPIA). [C-439/19 dejure](https://dejure.org/dienste/vernetzung/rechtsprechung?Aktenzeichen=C-439%2F19&Datum=22.06.2021&Gericht=EuGH)
  - C-34/21 line of authority also undermines reliance on § 26(1) BDSG general clause for special categories; whistleblower data processing (HinSchG § 10: internal reporting channels process personal data — legal basis is HinSchG itself, not GDPR consent).
- **Consent in employment (Art. 7 GDPR):** consent must be freely given; imbalance of power presumed in employment (EDPB Guidelines 5/2020); § 26(2) BDSG requires explicit consent for special categories; refusal must not disadvantage the employee; consent clauses in the contract (pre-formulated, bundled with other terms) are presumptively invalid. **Drafting impact:** any surveillance/monitoring/health-data clause needs a specific legal basis beyond blanket consent.
- **Relevance to contract clauses:** the skill should flag: monitoring clauses (video, e-mail, GPS, keylogging) — only justified by concrete necessity + transparency + (usually) works agreement, per BAG 29.6.2017 – 2 AZR 597/16 and BVerfG 6.11.2019 – 1 BvR 16/13 (business e-mail monitoring requires clear rules); data-processing consent clauses must be unbundled and voluntary.

### 1.6 Adequate minimum wages — Directive (EU) 2022/2041 → MiLoG
- **Basis:** Directive (EU) 2022/2041 (in force 14.11.2022; transposition deadline 15.11.2024). Framework for adequacy of statutory minimum wages (Art. 5) and promotion of collective bargaining (Art. 4; coverage target ≥80%).
- **CJEU 11.11.2025, C-19/23 (Denmark v Parliament and Council):** directive **partially annulled** — Art. 5(2) and 5(4) (binding criteria for setting/updating statutory minimum wages: purchasing power, general wage level/distribution, growth rates, productivity) void for lack of competence (Art. 153(5) TFEU — "pay" excluded). Rest of directive valid; Art. 4 (collective bargaining promotion) upheld — no violation of the right to collective bargaining. Sources: [LTO analysis](https://www.lto.de/recht/hintergruende/h/eugh-c1923-urteil-eu-mindestlohn-richtlinie-teilweise-unwirksam); [Bundestag WD paper PDF](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/1127460/urteil-eugh-mindestlohnrichtlinie.pdf); [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/personal/arbeitsrecht/eugh-erklaert-mindestlohn-richtlinie-der-eu-in-teilen-fuer-nichtig_76_665066.html).
- **German implementation/effect:** MiLoG stands independently; Mindestlohnkommission raised minimum wage to **€13.90/h from 1.1.2026, €14.60/h from 1.1.2027** (Cabinet decision 29.10.2025). Collective-bargaining coverage in Germany ~49% (west) / ~44% (east) [unverified exact figure; IAB]; directive's 80% target not binding on Germany after C-19/23.
- **C-174/21 and C-477/20: [unverified] — could not confirm any employment-law CJEU cases with these numbers.** (C-174/21 does not appear in employment-law databases searched; C-477/20 not found as employment case.) The task spec's citations are likely misremembered. The confirmed minimum-wage-line case is C-19/23 (2025). Do not cite C-174/21 or C-477/20.

### 1.7 Posted workers — Directive 96/71/EC as amended by (EU) 2018/957 → AEntG; Rome I & Brussels I
- **Basis:** Directive 96/71/EC (posted workers) amended by Directive (EU) 2018/957 (in force 30.7.2020): posting ≥12 months (extendable 6) → all applicable host-state terms apply; equal pay for posted workers (host-state minimum wages incl. statutory and universally applicable collective agreements); subcontracted-chain liability; administrative controls (A1 certificates, notification).
- **German transposition:** Arbeitnehmer-Entsendegesetz (AEntG) — §§ 1–7 AEntG (working conditions incl. minimum wage and collective agreements extended by Rechtsverordnung under § 7 AEntG); §§ 8–11 AEntG (social security); § 16 AEntG posting notification (before start; 4-week validity); § 23c AEntG (2022) information duty to EU citizens recruited abroad. NachwG § 2(3) posting documentation (above).
- **Rome I — Regulation (EC) 593/2008, Art. 8:** individual employment contracts: (1) law chosen by parties; proviso — choice cannot deprive employee of protection of mandatory provisions of the law of the **habitual workplace** (Art. 8(2): law of country where employee habitually works, or Art. 8(3) where temporarily posted elsewhere); (4) closest-connection fallback; (1) proviso protects Art. 8(2)/(3) mandatory rules. **Art. 9 Rome I — overriding mandatory provisions** (e.g. MiLoG, ArbZG, KSchG core, AGG) apply regardless of choice of law. **Drafting impact:** choice-of-law clauses selecting a low-protection law for work habitually performed in Germany are ineffective as to mandatory German rules (Art. 8(1) proviso + Art. 9).
- **Brussels I recast — Regulation (EU) 1215/2012, Arts. 20–23:** section 5 = employment: employer may sue employee only in employee's domicile (Art. 22(1)); employee may sue employer in employer's domicile **or** the place where the employee habitually works or last worked (Art. 21(1)(b)(i)), or the engaging establishment (Art. 21(1)(b)(ii)); jurisdiction agreements only post-dispute or allowing employee additional forums (Art. 23). **Drafting impact:** forum-selection clauses in employment contracts are void except within Art. 23 bounds.
- **Habitual workplace determination (CJEU):** C-29/10 (Koelzsch, 15.3.2011): international road transport — habitual workplace = place where employee actually performs most working time, not merely registered base; C-384/10 (Voogsgeerd, 15.12.2011): for seafarers, "engaging establishment" under Brussels I. Posting does not shift habitual workplace (Art. 8(2) Rome I); Rome I Art. 8(3) covers temporary posting >12 months (D 2018/957 minimum 12 months).

### 1.8 Platform work — Directive (EU) 2024/2831
- **Basis:** Directive (EU) 2024/2831 (in force 1.12.2024; transposition **2.12.2026**). Rebuttable presumption of employment relationship (Art. 5) where platform controls working conditions (personal scope: self-employed and employees); algorithmic management transparency (Art. 6–12: information, human review of significant decisions, limits on automated monitoring/decision-making for personal data; workers' representatives rights); subcontractor-chain measures (Art. 3 — MS "appropriate measures").
- **German status (2026-08):** BMAS preparing Referentenentwurf (Bundestag answer 24.4.2026, hib 344/2026); **no published draft as of 2026-08**; government examining Art. 3 measures including a possible **direct-employment obligation (Direktanstellungsgebot)** for delivery platforms; deadline 2.12.2026 will likely be missed or met only at the last minute. The presumption of employment will interact with German status-testing doctrine (arbeitnehmerähnliche Person; BAG "Fahrerkarten"-line on crowdworkers) and § 611a BGB criteria. Sources: [Bundestag hib 344/2026](https://www.bundestag.de/presse/hib/kurzmeldungen-1167478); [Gleiss Lutz](https://www.gleisslutz.com/de/know-how/update-eu-richtlinie-zur-verbesserung-der-arbeitsbedingungen-der-plattformarbeit-kraft-getreten); [Uni Hamburg](https://www.jura.uni-hamburg.de/die-fakultaet/aktuelle-meldungen/2026-07-27.html).

### 1.9 Whistleblowing — Directive (EU) 2019/1937 → HinSchG
- **Basis:** Directive (EU) 2019/1937 (deadline 17.12.2021; extended for <250 employees to 17.12.2023).
- **German transposition:** Hinweisgeberschutzgesetz (HinSchG, in force 2.7.2023): § 1 personal scope; § 2 material scope (EU-law violations; after 17.12.2023 extended to federal/national law — BVerfG 2 BvR 1817/23 partially suspended extension to constitutional-law violations); **§ 3(6) HinSchG** defines retaliation; **§ 36 HinSchG** burden of proof (presumption of retaliation); **§ 39 HinSchG: agreements restricting whistleblower rights are void** — "Vereinbarungen, die die nach diesem Gesetz bestehenden Rechte … einschränken, sind unwirksam." This voids confidentiality clauses barring reports; **§ 37(1) HinSchG**: retaliation prohibited; **§§ 12–18**: internal reporting channels (employers 50+ employees mandatory; 2023 threshold lowered from 249); §§ 19–24 external channels (BAF/Behörde); §§ 25–31 procedures.
- **Drafting impact:** contract clauses obliging employees to maintain confidentiality "in all circumstances," gag clauses tied to settlement agreements, or clauses penalizing reports are void per § 39 HinSchG; the skill must flag them. Sources: [§ 39 HinSchG gesetze-im-internet](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/hinschg/__39.html); [§ 3 HinSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/hinschg/__3.html); [ADVANT Beiten](https://www.advant-beiten.com/aktuelles/schweigsame-hinweisgeber-auswirkungen-des-hinweisgeberschutzgesetzes-auf-vertraulichkeitsvereinbarungen).

### 1.10 Pay Transparency Directive (EU) 2023/970 — NEW, priority item
- **Basis:** Directive (EU) 2023/970 (in force 6.6.2023; **transposition deadline 7.6.2026**). Content: Art. 4 gender-neutral pay structures (objective, gender-neutral criteria; job evaluation); Art. 5 **pay-range disclosure in job ads and before interview**; Art. 6 no questions about previous pay; Art. 7 right to information on pay levels (median) of comparator group + **Art. 7(6): agreements restricting disclosure of one's own pay are void/unenforceable**; Art. 8 reporting on gender pay gap (≥250 employees: annually; 150–249: every 3 years; 100–149: every 3 years) incl. pay-gap by category; Art. 9 joint pay assessments (gap ≥5% unjustified); Art. 10 right of equal-work/equal-value comparison; Art. 13 burden of proof on employer (indicia → shift); remedies incl. compensation.
- **German status (2026-08): deadline missed. No Referentenentwurf as of late May 2026; first draft expected Q1 2026 (Deloitte) did not materialize; realistic earliest national act end-2026/2027.** Existing law: EntgTranspG (2017): § 3 prohibition of gender pay discrimination, § 4 equal pay, §§ 10 ff. individual information right (only employers >200 employees, every 3 years), § 4(5) presumption of adequacy for collective-agreement pay systems, §§ 17 ff. reporting (500+ employees). The directive will require lowering thresholds, annual reporting, pay-range disclosure in ads, and deleting § 4(5) presumption or narrowing it.
- **Effect from 8.6.2026 without transposition (PWWL analysis):** public employers bound directly by sufficiently precise directive provisions (vertical direct effect); private employers: no horizontal direct effect, but — Art. 157 TFEU equal-pay principle applies horizontally; courts must interpret EntgTranspG, AGG, BGB, BetrVG, TVG in conformity with Directive 2023/970; Art. 4 criteria become the interpretive yardstick for pay structures; § 22 AGG burden of proof read more employee-favorably; Art. 7(6) used to strike pay-secrecy clauses via § 307 BGB; **new organizational duties (Art. 5 ad disclosure, Art. 8 reporting, Art. 9 joint assessments) cannot be created by judicial interpretation** — those await legislation. First reporting dates per the directive's own timeline (2027 for reporting year 2026) may shift to 2028 in the German act [unverified whether German act will postpone].
- **Drafting impact (contract offers):** salary bands in job ads; no prior-pay questions; no pay-secrecy clauses; documentation of pay decisions; comparison-group transparency. Sources: [PWWL 8.6.2026](https://pwwl.de/eu-entgelttransparenzrichtlinie-ab-dem-8-juni-2026-was-gilt-auch-ohne-deutsches-umsetzungsgesetz/); [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/de/de/services/tax/perspectives/eu-entgelttransparenzrichtlinie.html); [Personio](https://www.personio.de/hr-lexikon/entgelttransparenzgesetz/); [reteach timeline](https://www.reteach.com/eu-entgelttransparenzrichtlinie-2026/); [afa-anwalt](https://www.afa-anwalt.de/news/entgelttransparenzgesetz-2026-rl-2023-970/).

## 3. Cross-border contracts: applicable law, jurisdiction, posting, flagging rules

- **Applicable law — Rome I (EC) 593/2008, Art. 8:** default = law of habitual workplace (Art. 8(2)); temporary posting elsewhere does not change it (Art. 8(3) applies law of sending state where posting is temporary and a close connection exists — note: 2018/957 aligns host-state *pay* rules on top); party choice allowed (Art. 8(1)) **but cannot deprive the employee of mandatory protection of the habitual-workplace law**; closest-connection fallback (Art. 8(4)). **Art. 9 Rome I: overriding mandatory provisions** of the forum (and of the place of performance) apply regardless — German MiLoG, ArbZG health limits, AGG core, KSchG (as overriding public policy, per BAG practice), § 613a BGB transfer rules are treated as such. **Skill flag:** choice-of-law clauses selecting non-German law for German-habitual work are ineffective as to mandatory German protections; clause is not per se void but its protective effect fails.
- **Jurisdiction — Brussels I recast (EU) 1215/2012, Arts. 20–23:** employee-friendly forum rules (see 1.7). **Skill flag:** forum-selection clauses favoring employer's home court are void except post-dispute agreements or Art. 23-compliant widening of employee options.
- **Habitual workplace determination:** C-29/10 Koelzsch (actual performance center of gravity), C-384/10 Voogsgeerd (engaging establishment), C-266/14 (Tyco, travel for no-fixed-workplace workers = working time under 2003/88). **Skill flag:** contracts designating a "registered office" or "mailbox" as workplace do not defeat the actual-workplace test.
- **Posting:** AEntG notification + A1; host-state minimum wage and universally applicable collective agreements apply from day 1 (2018/957); >12 (extendable to 18) months → all host terms; NachwG § 2(2)/(3) documentation; § 23c AEntG information duty. **Skill flag:** pure "letterbox posting" (no genuine service provision) is abuse; A1 fraud affects criminal liability but not civil working conditions.
- **Dual employment:** parallel contracts with different employers lawful absent exclusivity clauses (1.3); each contract assessed separately; working-time limits aggregate across employers under EU law (CJEU C-684/19? [unverified]; Art. 6(2) Directive 2003/88 48h average refers to all employers; German ArbZG § 2(1) definition of working time covers all employment relationships — BAG 21.6.2016? [unverified]); MiLoG applies per employer-hour.
- **What the skill should flag:** (i) choice-of-law clauses attempting to avoid mandatory German law → void effect via Art. 8(1) proviso + Art. 9 Rome I; (ii) jurisdiction clauses in employer's favor → void via Art. 23 Brussels I; (iii) posting without A1/notification; (iv) absence of NachwG posting documentation; (v) habitual-workplace vs. contract-form designation conflicts.

## 4. Interaction with German law — where DE exceeds EU, where EU constrains DE

**German law exceeds EU minimums (national protective layer — the skill must not treat EU floors as German ceilings):**
- **AGB review (§§ 305–310 BGB)** — purely national, no EU analogue for employment contracts; catch-all clause control (transparency, § 307) beyond directive floors.
- **KSchG** — unfair-dismissal protection (social justification, § 1 KSchG) far exceeds EU minimum (EU has no dismissal-protection directive; only non-discrimination and specific protections); § 613a BGB transfer-of-undertaking protection implements Directive 2001/23/EC but with national additions; **EU Directive 2001/23/EC** itself binds § 613a interpretation (CJEU C-396/07 Juuri; C-561/11 Feyerbacher).
- **ArbZG 8h/10h daily rule vs EU 48h weekly average** — German daily cap stricter; German opt-out from 48h average (Art. 22 Directive 2003/88) **not** used (ArbZG has no individual opt-out — EU's optional opt-out was not transposed; weekly average binds via § 3 ArbZG as interpreted).
- **BUrlG** — 24 working days statutory (4 weeks), exceeds EU's 4 weeks only in measurement; EU Art. 7 leaves German carry-over law (15-month rule via CJEU case law, BAG 9 AZR 401/19) under EU pressure.
- **MuSchG, EntgFG (continued pay), BetrVG codetermination, TzBfG detail** — national layers beyond directives.
- **EntgTranspG thresholds** (>200 employees, 3-year intervals) exceed EU 2023/970's stricter requirements — EU law now *constrains* the German statute (conforming interpretation; thresholds themselves cannot be judicially lowered, PWWL).

**EU law constrains German rules:**
- **Befristung limits:** CJEU fixed-term doctrine (Adeneler, Grupo Norte) requires effective sanctions for successive contracts; § 14(1)/14(2) TzBfG limits are national implementation of clause 5 Framework Agreement; the planned 2026 reform (48 months/6 renewals) will be assessed against clause 5 — a CJEU challenge is foreseeable. (No pending case confirmed [unverified].)
- **§ 14(2) s.2 TzBfG Vorbeschäftigungsverbot** — national strictness; EU does not require it; 2026 reform partly relaxes (retirement-age hires, 1.1.2026; 48-month rule 2026–2030).
- **Pay transparency 2023/970 overrides EntgTranspG if not transposed** — no direct horizontal effect, but conforming interpretation + Art. 157 TFEU; public employers bound directly from 8.6.2026; § 4(5) EntgTranspG presumption of adequacy for collective-agreement pay under pressure.
- **Working-time recording (C-55/18)** — BAG derived obligation from ArbSchG; a future ArbZG amendment cannot reduce below the CJEU floor.
- **AGG § 22 burden of proof** — implements 2000/78/EC Art. 10, 2006/54/EC Art. 19, 2000/43/EC Art. 8; CJEU interprets indicia threshold (C-109/88 Danfoss; C-415/10 Meister; C-83/14 CHEZ).
- **Employee data (C-34/21)** — § 26 BDSG general clause cannot cover special categories; national works-agreement practice must adapt.
- **Platform directive 2024/2831 (from 2.12.2026)** — will constrain German status-testing and require algorithmic-transparency rules regardless of the national reform debate.
